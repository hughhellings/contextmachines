# Changelog

What changed in each release, for the people running it. Versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org); while it is 0.x, a minor version may change what it prints, its options or the shape of `map.json`, and says so here.

## Unreleased

- **Dates go into date boxes the way the box takes them.** A date the AI writes as people do ("2026-11-02 09:30", "9:30 pm", 2026/11/2) is written the way a browser's own date, time or date-and-time box takes it, instead of the fill being refused and tried again. A date that could be day or month first is never guessed.
- **A test signs in with your account even when its goal doesn't say "sign in".** In the app, a goal like "add an invoice" on a site behind a sign-in runs as the project's account when it is clear which one (the only one, the default, or one the goal names), instead of meeting the sign-in page with no account and stopping. A goal about the sign-in page, signing out or a sign-in meant to fail still starts signed out, and with several accounts and none clear it never guesses.
- **Forms are filled like a real user fills them.** A step that creates or adds a record fills every field and every row the form shows by default, not only the required ones, with values that fit together (a later row later in time, the opposite side where rows have one), and reads the form's own status ("1 still open", "0 of 2"). It leaves alone what a real user would: search and filter boxes, uploads, passwords, card details, codes, promo codes, switched-off sections, honeypots and fields whose note says they aren't part of the record. Right before the save, the engine reads the form with no AI; if fields a real user would fill are still empty, the press is held once (per form, at most twice a step) and the actor is told which. It never presses or types anything itself and is never a verdict. Each step records `fill: {shown, filled, empty}` (labels only, never values) and shows "filled 15 of 15 fields shown" in the terminal, the report and the app.
- **A step that only opens a form is no longer read as filling or saving.** Words that say which way to add something ("if it offers a choice of how to add", "pick the option to enter a contact by hand") made a plan be refused ("Couldn't run: the AI's plan wasn't usable") or a run end NOT SURE right after the form opened. A step that really types or saves still needs its own check. And if a plan is still refused only because a step with no check reads as doing something, that step is joined onto the next one, which keeps its check, instead of the run ending.
- **What the tester should know about this site.** Plain facts about how your site works ("An order has two lines: what was bought and what was returned. Add test orders to the Test account, not a verified customer account.") in one box per project: Settings in the app, or `contextmachines/context.md` in the terminal (the same file), plus `--context "<text>"` to add to it for one run. The planner, the actor and both judges read it with every run, fenced after their own rules: it can't switch off any rule, make a step pass without the page showing it, or loosen a verdict (a judge may only be stricter). At most 4,000 characters, refused rather than cut; a text that looks like it holds a password, key or token, or one of your stored values, is refused. Runs record only its length and a SHA-256, never the words; a saved test that passed with other notes (or none) is planned again from its goal. After a NOT SURE or BLOCKED run, the result offers "Tell the tester about this site" with the run's reason.
- **A refused save the site explains is tried again with the form's other options.** When a save or submit is refused for a reason the page gives ("This account is verified: its records come only from the provider…") and the form has a choice about it with other options (an Account select), the tester goes back, picks another and saves again, at most twice a step, before giving up. Never an option that creates something new (unless your notes plainly say it may: a "never" or "don't" about creating wins), costs or moves money, deletes, closes or archives something, or reads like someone else's or shared; never for a payment or a money transfer, a sign-in, a paywall, a bot check, a permission on a site that isn't yours, or the site's own error ("try again", maintenance, a 5xx), which stays NOT SURE. A refusal stands only until the next save: a save that worked is never followed by another try. The page's words reach the AI fenced as page data. With no such option it ends BLOCKED with the new code `SEED_DATA_MISSING` and what to do: add what the site needs, or say which to use in What the tester should know about this site. The tries are in `result.json` (`recoveries`).

- **The sign-in is shown, its boxes as dots.** Signing in as a test account (saved steps with no AI, the sign-in again after a session ended, `test --setup`, or a test whose own steps sign in) streams to the live screen and is recorded like the rest of the run: the real sign-in page, with every box a stored value is typed into (username or email, password, `{x.totp}`, an emailed code, a `--var`) drawn as dots. Before the first key every text box on the page is masked and read back painted in every frame; afterwards the box typed into stays dots, and so does one the page draws again in its place (a React re-render, a show-password toggle that makes it `type=text`). The "Typing a stored value" card is now only the fallback where the mask can't be seen painted first (an inline `!important` rule, a frame that can't be read). Page words that echo a password, code or key still get the card, and so does any change in the page's own words while a password, code, key or `--var` is being typed (a code page whose slots show each digit, a suggestion list that repeats what is typed); a code made at type time (`{x.totp}`, an emailed code) is looked for from before its first key. Just before the first key the box it goes into is read back as dots again, or the card goes up. The browser's own "fill out this field" bubble is never shown for a box holding a stored value. The account's name or email in the page's own words still shows. A test that signed in just before keeps the sign-in's video beside its own as `sign-in.webm` ("Watch the sign-in" in the app and the report), and its video note says so. The AI's pictures are unchanged: none in a run that may type a stored value.
- **Signed-in runs keep their live screen and video again, with the cover.** A run as a test account, or one that types a `--var` value, keeps its live screen, `run.webm` and step photos. What could show a stored value is covered by the page itself, so every capture gets it: the sign-in is never shown (the live screen says "Signing in as <label>…", and the video opens on a "Signed in as <label>" card); from just before a stored value (an account's username or password, `{x.totp}`, an emailed code, a `--var`) is typed until no field shows it, a "Typing a stored value" card stands over the page, drawn before the first key; and password, card and code fields' text is drawn transparent in every frame (superseded above: boxes as dots, the sign-in shown). Before every frame, a guard in the page compares its fields and words with the run's stored values (as salted hashes) and covers any frame that would show one, such as a form the site draws again with the value in it. The result and `report.html` say what the video skips. The AI's pictures keep their rule: none in a run that may type a stored value. Words the page itself writes (its header's "Signed in as jane@acme.io") do show.
- **Login checks the way e2e and TesterArmy do them: a run never waits for a person.** The pop-up that paused a run in the app for a code, a sign-in link or a captcha is gone. Instead, **Set up sign-in** (under Accounts, for any account; `npx contextmachines accounts setup <label> --url <site>` in the terminal) opens a real browser window on your computer where you sign in once yourself: type the code from your email or phone, open the magic link, solve a captcha or sign in with Google. The signed-in check is measured, the session saved, and later runs (app, terminal, CI, schedules) reuse it with no one there. A run whose session is missing or no longer works ends BLOCKED with the fix "Set up sign-in". Authenticator codes and a local mail catcher still need no one.
- **Every blocked sign-in in the app is a run.** It is saved in Runs history and the report with verdict BLOCKED, its code and fix line, and the later steps not run, never only "couldn't run" (an account that isn't there, a host not confirmed, a sign-up or test code on a host that isn't yours included).
- **New codes:** `DEVICE_CHECK` ("check your email to confirm it's you": the sign-in isn't sent again), `SMS_CODE_NEEDED` (a code by text, with its own fix: a QA account without one, or the provider's test mode), `TEST_CODE_NOT_ALLOWED`, `BOT_CHECK_NEEDS_PERSON` (a captcha in front of signing in on a host that isn't yours; on yours it stays `ACCESS_TOKEN_REQUIRED`, never `AUTH_SESSION_EXPIRED`).
- **Sign-in provider test codes.** `"codes": [{ "testCode": "424242" }]` on an account types the provider's fixed test code (Clerk's test mode) where the page asks for a code, on a host marked as yours only; anywhere else it is refused before the browser opens. With `"clerk"` under `"sites"`, Clerk's Testing Token also gets a sign-in past Clerk's bot protection on your own host.

- **Sign in with your own account on any site.** An account no longer needs the site marked as yours to sign in: say once that it's your own account there (`npx contextmachines accounts sign-in <host>`, or yes when `test --as` asks, `--yes` with no one to ask, or the confirm in the app's Accounts). That is kept in `.auth/signin-ok.json` and never makes the site yours: sign-ups, test payments and site access still need `accounts mine`, and the tester still never posts, pays, sends or deletes. With your own account on a site that isn't yours, it also doesn't vote, rate, report, apply, add to a cart or change the account's security settings. An account's values are now typed only on the exact host they're for (port included): a sign-in that goes to another host or port of the same site types nothing there until you say that host is where you sign in too, and your account on a site that isn't yours is never typed on another site. A sign-in page (`signInPage`) must be a path on the site itself. A code by email or text we can't read ends BLOCKED `CODE_NEEDS_PERSON` before the browser opens, so no code is sent that no one can type (Set up sign-in, above, is the fix). `--allow-host` with a port (`localhost:4000`) allows that port only. **Set up sign-in** under Accounts in the app opens a window on your computer for SSO and Google. New codes: `CODE_NEEDS_PERSON`, `SIGN_IN_NOT_CONFIRMED`. Accounts can be `"first": "sms-code"`.
- **`contextmachines mcp`: the tester for your coding agent.** An MCP server on stdio with five tools (`suggest_tasks`, `run_test`, `run_saved`, `get_result`, `list_tests`): `claude mcp add contextmachines -- npx contextmachines mcp --ai claude-code`. Each test runs as a child `test` process, signed out, one at a time; a client can't pass values to type, sign in, show the browser or change the server's options, and only verdicts, reasons and paths come back. A run longer than 50 seconds hands back a run id for `get_result`; cancelling, closing the connection or Ctrl-C stops the run and its browser.
- **An accessibility scan on every run.** Each same-site page a run reaches is checked once against eight rules (no name, no alt text, no language, no label, placeholder-only label, heading order, duplicate ids, small targets), measured by the browser with no AI and no extra library, with WCAG's own exceptions. In `report.html` under "Accessibility", `result.json` (`accessibility`), the web view, `--explore`'s page, the MCP result and one line in the terminal. It never changes a verdict.
- **A judged check, labelled everywhere.** When no measured check fits (a total being right, what a chart draws), the planner may write `judged {claim}`: a separate AI ask that sees only the final screen says whether it holds, and its "holds" counts only when it quotes lines that are really on the page. A page with words aimed at the judge is never believed; anything unsure is NOT SURE. A judged pass says "judged by AI" in the terminal, the report, `result.json`, JUnit, the web view, the saved test and the MCP result, and exits 0. No verdict is ever saved: a saved judged step asks again on replay. A saved test with a judged step can't be read by an older version.
- **A second opinion for the planner's guesses.** When the planner's own guessed check (an address part, a title, words not in your goal or steps) fails on the last step after the step finished (the AI said done, or stuck on that check), the judge is asked once with your goal's words; only a "holds" with quotes found on the page passes it, labelled, with the check it overruled shown. A check you asked for is never second-guessed. `--measured-only` (test, ui) turns judged checks and second opinions off.
- **Final fixes before 0.2.0.** The SDK's `agent.act()` never presses a send past an unsolved captcha box (BLOCKED, as `test` does). A run as a test account keeps no video, live screen or photos, saved replay or not. A failed AI ask with no usage still counts against `--max-tokens`. In the web view: an Assert card (or any card you edited) is never overruled by a second opinion; an Assert card marked "judged" is really judged by the AI; a judged step's pill, card and result say "judged"; `ui --measured-only` plans no judged check; a saved test keeps its `identity` when saved again; a captcha box read twice shows one card; `report.html` says when a person passed a bot check by hand. A judged check on a page still changing after the step is judged once more when it changes.
- **An address that holds on a "not found" page isn't a pass.** A step whose address or title check holds while the page's title or heading says it isn't there ("Symbol not found") is NOT SURE, unless the goal is about such a page.
- **Pictures of the screen, secrets blacked out.** For a chart, a canvas or a page with nothing listed, the actor and the judge may get one JPEG of the screen, with every field that could hold a secret blacked out and the blackout checked in the bytes sent. Never in a run that keeps no video, never when a secret shows as text, never written to disk. Claude Code, the Anthropic API and OpenAI-compatible vision models take them; Codex's `--image` is built but not tried yet.
- **Test payments, `test --pay`.** One test may pay in a provider's test mode (Stripe, PayPal sandbox, Paddle sandbox, Shopify's test gateway) on a host you've marked as yours. Live (or not shown to be test) ends BLOCKED on sight, and a payment is never tried twice in a run. Off by default; a visitor's test never pays.
- **Saved tests keep working on a new address of the same app.** Name it once with `"identity"` in `contextmachines.config.json`: tests are filed under that name and replay, heal and save on any of its addresses (a preview). A saved navigation on the test's own site is now saved as a path.
- **CI: `action/ci`.** A GitHub Action that runs saved tests on every pull request or Vercel preview: replays with no AI, asks an AI only for tests that need it, writes JUnit and the job summary, keeps one PR comment up to date, and sets a commit status a Vercel Deployment Check can wait for. Templates are in `action/ci/workflows/`.
- **Tests in code: `contextmachines/sdk`.** Mixes exact Playwright steps with agent steps (`agent.act`, `agent.assert`), with the same measured checks, safety stops, secrets by handle and no-AI replay as `contextmachines test`.
- **The web view is now an app.** `npx contextmachines ui` opens on "What do you want to test?": put in a live or local address and it becomes a project. A left rail holds Tests, Runs, Issues, Accounts and Settings. Describe a test in a sentence and get step cards (act, assert, login) you can edit, switch off and reorder before anything runs; Run plays exactly those cards, and a pass is replayed next time with no AI. Every run is kept in Runs with its video. Issues lists what runs measured (failed checks, console and page errors, 4xx and 5xx, friction, accessibility), deduped across runs, with a prompt to copy into your coding agent. Test the whole site explores the project's address (signed out, that origin only) and files what it finds. Schedules replay saved tests (no AI) while the app is open, one run at a time. When a check stands in the way, the run ends BLOCKED; it is never solved for you (pass it once yourself in Set up sign-in). The old page is at `/classic`. A Mac notification says when a run fails (switch it off in Settings).
- **A spend cap.** `--max-tokens` and `--max-usd` (or `CONTEXTMACHINES_MAX_TOKENS` / `_USD`) cap what a run's AI may take. In CI or a schedule, an AI billed per token without a cap is refused before the run starts. The local cloud marks its runs as unwatched and takes a project's "Most AI tokens per run".
- API AIs retry a rate limit or a brief outage (429, 5xx, 529) up to 3 times, honouring Retry-After; errors never print a key.
- `--ai google` uses Gemini's own API and `--ai ollama` Ollama's own, holding answers to the JSON Schema; `OLLAMA_HOST` is read. API AIs can take pictures with a question.
- New default models, as the providers list them today: OpenAI `gpt-6.1-sol` and `gpt-6.1-terra` (gpt-5 shuts down on 11 December), Gemini `gemini-3.8-flash` and `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` (2.5 is closed to new users). The Vercel AI Gateway and OpenRouter defaults now use their real ids, `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5` and `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` (the old dashed ones don't exist there). OpenAI's own API gets the instructions as a `developer` message, as its docs say.
- **Your own site behind a firewall or a bot check.** `"sites"` in `contextmachines.config.json` names what its owner issued for a host: a Vercel bypass secret, a Cloudflare Access service token, a basic-auth login, one header of your own, a Clerk Testing Token (made with a `sk_test_` key), a marker. Sent only to that exact host, once you've marked it as yours, and never on a redirect elsewhere; the Clerk token only to `/v1/` on your Clerk Frontend API. Store them with `accounts site …` or `CM_SITE_*`. A bot check that still stays is BLOCKED `ACCESS_TOKEN_REQUIRED` on your own host; a basic-auth login is `AUTH_BASIC_REQUIRED`, Vercel's protection `VERCEL_BYPASS_REQUIRED`.
- **Google, Microsoft, Apple and GitHub sign-ins, by hand.** Nothing is ever typed on a provider's page. `test --setup --by-hand --as <label>` opens a window, you sign in, and only your app's own cookies are kept. A "Continue with …" handoff to an allowed provider can be tested, and a sign-in window the page opens there is left to answer it. `learn --login` now drops a provider's cookies too.
- **Test accounts in the web view.** A Test accounts panel beside the Website box: mark the site as yours, add an account, set its password and authenticator secret (only "set" ever shows again), see the accounts sign-ups made. Pick the Account a goal or the ticked tasks run as; the live screen is hidden while it signs in, and a chip says who the run is signed in as.
- **`accounts site` and `accounts created`.** Store a site's owner values; list the accounts sign-up tests made.

- **Sign in with a code or a link, and sign up.** A test account may sign in with an authenticator code (made on your machine from its stored secret when typed), or a code or sign-in link by email read from a mail catcher on your own machine (Mailpit's API: `"codes": [{ "catcher": "http://127.0.0.1:8025" }]`). The AI sees only `{admin.totp}`, `{admin.email_code}` or `{admin.magic_link}`; a replay gets a new code. No message from a working inbox is FAIL `CODE_NOT_RECEIVED`; an inbox that doesn't answer is BLOCKED `CODE_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`. An account whose first step is `"sign-up"` makes a new account each run with `--sign-up` (a host marked as yours, never on live, test addresses only), listed in `.auth/created.jsonl`.
- **The one "send" a test may press.** Signed in as a test account on a host you've marked as yours or confirmed you sign in on, a button whose whole name is "Send code", "Send me a link", "Resend code", "Send verification code" or the like may be pressed, in a form that asks only who signs in, at most 3 times a run. Never a reset or an invite, never in a visitor's test.
- **Faster runs: fewer AI asks.** The AI may give up to 3 more actions after the one it picks (open the search, type, press Enter). Each is done only after the browser looks again, and the rest are dropped when one fails, the page goes elsewhere or its element is gone. A step whose check holds after an action now passes with no "done" asked, unless the check held before the step or holds on words the test typed itself.
- **More verbs.** Back, scroll inside a list or panel, scroll to words on the page, double-click, right-click, check and uncheck. Each meets the same site and buy/post rules as a click. Back never sends a form again. A tab the page opens on the same site is still followed in the test's own tab.
- **`test --phone`.** Runs a goal at a phone's width (390 × 844, touch, the mobile layout), and records the video at that size. The web view shows a portrait screen in a phone-shaped frame.

## 0.2.0 - 2026-11-16

`test` is the new headline: put in a URL (or one sentence), a real browser does the steps, and the browser (never the AI) says PASS or FAIL. `learn` is unchanged, and now leaves you starter tests.

- **A URL alone.** `npx contextmachines test --url https://en.wikipedia.org` looks at the site with no AI (the home page and a few pages its menu links to, the cookie banner answered "no"), asks your AI once for 5 to 10 tasks a visitor would do there, and lets you tick the ones to run (Space, `a` for all, Enter, `q`). Each runs live with its own checklist, a pass is saved as a test, and it ends with `Tests 2 passed · 0 failed · 0 not sure` and where people got stuck across them. In a pipe it prints the numbered list (`--json` too); `--pick 1,3` or `--all` runs them. With tests saved for that site, `--url` runs them, and `--suggest` asks again. Suggested tasks are a signed-out visitor's: nothing that signs in, signs up, comments, likes, follows, subscribes, posts, buys or sends is offered or pressed.
- **Suggested tasks stay a visitor's, on the site.** A task that ends on another site (a link out, another language's address), or that votes, rates, reports, follows, joins, orders or signs in with another account, is never suggested; the tests already saved for the site aren't suggested again. A task stopped or left when the budget runs out is said as "not run", and the run doesn't exit 0. A check on an address that holds only the words the test typed passes with a note that it proves little.
- **`--explore` tests the whole site.** It runs every suggested task with no picking on one budget (`--minutes`, 10 by default) and writes a page for the site, `runs/<host>-explore-<time>/index.html`, linking each task's report, with where people get stuck across all of them.
- **The web view: `npx contextmachines ui` (or `test --ui`).** A local page, side by side: the steps on the left, each opening to what it is doing with a spinner that turns into a tick or a cross, a timer and Stop test (which closes the browser at once); the live browser on the right in a window frame (Chromium's screencast, or a photo after each action where that can't start). When a test ends, the right side becomes its video with a Go to for every step and action, and a PASS or FAIL card shows the screenshots and where people get stuck. Suggest tasks, tick them, run them or explore the site, or run your saved tests. Same engine, safety rules and files as the terminal, whose output is unchanged. It listens on 127.0.0.1 only, with a random token in its address; every request checks the token and the Host header, changes are POSTs with the token in a header, and the page loads nothing from the web. `--no-open` prints the address only.
- **A site behind a bot check says so.** Cloudflare's "Just a moment...", a CAPTCHA, DataDome, Akamai or DDoS-Guard in front of a site is found with no AI and waited on for up to about 10 seconds each time a fresh browser opens the site, in case it clears by itself; it is never answered or got past. If it stays, the look suggests nothing and says `<host> checks for bots (Cloudflare's bot check) and didn't let the test browser in` (exit 3, and a card with its photo in the web view), and a test that meets one ends BLOCKED, "Blocked by the site's bot check" (exit 4), not FAIL, with the rest of that site's tasks, or saved tests, not run. A bot check's own control ("Verify you are human", "Press & Hold") is never pressed, even where the page wasn't found to be a wall. No task about waiting for or passing a security check is suggested.
- **Real sites like YouTube.** Consent walls are answered "no" as the site opens (Google's and YouTube's consent pages count as the site for that step only); slow pages and players get longer waits; an ad's link to another site is a click to try again, not a stop. Two new checks measure a video: `video_playing` (its time moves on) and `video_paused` (it has played, and its time stands still).
- **`learn` runs where `test` has.** It counts `runs/`, `tests/` and `.auth/` in `contextmachines/` as its own, and when it writes no starter tests it says to try `npx contextmachines test --url <site>` to pick tasks.
- **"Made-up" is said every time.** A value the AI wrote into a step of its plan, not in your goal, your steps or a `--var`, is marked made-up in the output and the saved test, as a value it made up for a field is.
- **`test`: test any web app with one sentence.** `npx contextmachines test "open the docs and search for locator" --url https://playwright.dev` plans the steps, does them in a real browser and passes each only when the browser measures its check. You get PASS or FAIL, a video and a report with a "go to" per step; a pass is saved and replays with no AI. It stays on the site and never presses anything that pays, sends or deletes.
- **`test` fills what the goal didn't say with plainly made-up values.** A form that also wants an email, an address or a reference number gets `test+cm@example.com`, `1 Test Street`, `TEST-001`, marked "made-up"; a password, a code, card or bank details are never made up, and the step says which `--var` to pass.
- **`test` with no goal runs every saved test.** `npx contextmachines test` replays everything under `contextmachines/tests/`, prints each test's steps and `Tests 5 passed · 1 failed · 0 not sure`, and exits 0 only when all passed. `--grep` picks some, `--junit <file>` writes JUnit XML for CI, and `--url` runs them all on another origin (a preview build). A step whose element has gone is healed and flagged where there's an AI, and not sure where there isn't.
- **Log in once.** `test --setup "log in with {email} and {password}" --url … --var email=… --var password=…` saves the browser session to `contextmachines/.auth/<host>.json` (owner-only, git-ignored, never printed), and later tests on that host start signed in (`--signed-out` to start without). Values come from `--var` or `CM_VAR_<NAME>`, and are masked everywhere; the sign-in keeps no video or photos.
- **Where a new user might get stuck.** Each test run notes the pop-ups it had to close, steps that took more than 3 actions or a second try, controls with no name it had to use, validation errors the page showed and steps that waited over 5 seconds: in the report, in `result.json` as `friction`, and as one line in the terminal. Measured, not an AI's opinion.
- **`learn` writes your first tests.** For each journey it walked to its end, `learn` writes a starter test under `contextmachines/tests/<host>/` (the saved-test JSON with `"draft": true`), never over a file that's there, and ends with `Wrote 3 starter tests · run them: npx contextmachines test`. A draft knows what was pressed and checks the address each step should reach; its first run has your AI do the steps (a form gets made-up values), and that pass saves it as a plain test that replays with no AI. Run with no AI, a draft's steps are "not sure" and say so. `map.json`'s finished journeys now say the screen they `ends` on.
- **The GitHub Action runs your saved tests.** `tests: true` runs `contextmachines test --junit` on the tests committed under `contextmachines/tests/` (or `tests-dir`), with no AI key needed for a replay; a step that needs healing is healed with `anthropic-api-key` and "not sure" without it. Pass and fail go to the job summary, one row per test, the JUnit file is the `junit` output, and the job fails unless every test passed. `url` then runs every test on that origin (a preview deploy), and `{name}` values come from `CM_VAR_<NAME>` in the step's env. The learn-and-compare mode is unchanged.
- **Your Claude Code or Codex setup stays out of every ask.** Claude Code runs in an empty folder in safe mode, so no `CLAUDE.md`, auto-memory, hooks or project details reach the model; Codex reads no `AGENTS.md`. Claude Code still tells the model your account's email, so the tester is told to make up only generic test names (see PRIVACY.md).
- **What `test` won't make up, or send.** A sign-in (`--setup`), or a goal that names a `{value}`, makes nothing up; an email or user name next to a password, a message box, and a field a `--var` was given for never get a made-up value. A form that sends a message is refused whatever its button says ("Submit" on a contact form). A saved test keeps only the try that worked when a send failed and was tried again. A sign-in passes when its form has gone unless the goal says what to check.
- **Healing is said only when it happened.** With no AI, a gone element is "not sure" and says there was no AI, and nothing claims a heal or a rewritten file. A heal shows only the action that failed and its replacement, renames the element in the step's words, and is counted in the suite's `Tests` line and marked in JUnit, with a reminder to commit it. A check that proves little is counted there too.
- **A draft must prove something.** A draft step that did nothing, or whose check held before it, stays a draft and is "not sure"; `learn` writes no starter whose check would hold on its first screen, and says why when it writes none. A saved test whose last step checks nothing isn't read.
- **Secrets stay out of what's written.** `--var` values are masked as a site echoes them (URL-encoded, `+` for a space, another case); a run that types one keeps no video or photos; a bypass token in `--url` stays out of `result.json`, JUnit and the saved test. On Windows, Claude Code and Codex start in a folder outside your profile, so your user name isn't in the path.
- **The suite's `--url` moves one site's tests.** Tests of several sites are refused (pick with `--grep`), and on another origin nothing is healed or saved. A test that lands on the sign-in form of a host with a saved session says to run `--setup` again. "Took N actions" counts filling and sending one form as one thing to do, and waiting on the test's own timeouts isn't counted as the page being slow.
- **It sees what is open first on a big page.** The AI used to get the first 6,000 characters of the page in order, so on a site like TradingView a search box, menu or dialog drawn at the end of the page was never shown, and clicking Search seemed to change nothing. Now the layer holding the focus comes first (its field marked `[focused]`), then open menus and dialogs, what is new since the last action, what is on screen and the rest, up to 10,000 characters (8,000 for the planner; less of the rest of the page on a second look within a step), with a last line saying how many elements aren't shown.
- **Clickable rows are numbered.** A plain row with no role (a search result, a language in a menu) is offered as an `item`, clicked by its number, refused like a button when its words read like buying or sending, and found again by its words on replay.
- **The element the AI named is the one pressed.** A live action uses that very element, and says it has gone ("look again") rather than press another of the same name. A saved test now records `"rules": 2` and, where its role and name alone wouldn't find an element again, how many matched, its test id, a link's address or its words: on replay one that more or fewer elements now match is healed (and flagged) or not sure, never clicked by its place. Tests saved before replay exactly as they did.
- **A covered click fails fast and says why.** It is tried without pressing first, so a click under an overlay fails in a few seconds instead of 8, saying what covers it ("an open layer ("Symbol, ISIN, or CUSIP" search box)"), and Playwright's own log never reaches the AI.
- **Each action says what it changed.** The step's history gets "→ opened: menu", "→ closed: menu", "→ went to …", "→ focus moved to …" or "→ no change on the page" after each action, in place of a note that said nothing changed when it had.
- **Going round in circles ends the step.** The same action on an unchanged page three times, a cycle of two to four actions done twice, seven scrolls in a row, or three actions that changed nothing is warned once, then ends the step, whose check is still measured. With two turns left the AI is told, and its last turn may only say done or stuck.
- **Styled checkboxes, hover menus and same-site frames.** A checkbox drawn hidden is clicked through its label (its words are read and refused first, and the box must change); `hover` opens a menu that opens only under the pointer; elements inside an embedded page of the same site can be used, and those of another site's frame never are.
- **A start page that says "not found".** Suggested tasks for an address that shows "not found" to a visitor start from the site's home page, and say so; a run given that address and a goal is only warned. `result.json` keeps where the run ended (`endUrl`) and every address it went to (`urls`), and the report says "Started at … · ended at …".
- **"Don't allow" is a no.** A cookie banner whose refusal reads "Don't allow" (either apostrophe) or "Do not allow" is answered with it; "Accept all" and "Allow all" are still never pressed.
- **Type where the focus is, and the search shortcuts.** `type {value}` types into what has focus (the search box a click or a shortcut just opened), with the same rules as `fill`: a password from a `--var` only and never in a visitor's test, a secret-shaped value nobody gave refused, a comment box refused by its name. `Control+K`, `Meta+K` and `/` may be pressed (`/` not while a text field has focus); a saved `type` focuses its field again on replay.
- **It waits for the page, not a fixed time.** After an action it waits for the requests the action started (in the next half second, up to 3 seconds) and the page it went to, so suggestions that arrive a second later are in the next look; a search box or combobox gets its last character as a key press, for sites that listen for keys. "Waited Xs on the page" counts only that time.
- **A check that holds before anything is done sends the plan back.** Before the run, each planned check is measured on the start page; one that already holds (a feed link with the very words the step looks for) proves nothing, so the AI is asked once for another plan. A second plan that repeats it runs, with the step flagged as proving little. This never passes or fails anything.
- **A guessed check is sent back too, and a step that did nothing can't pass.** Measured on the start page with no AI: a check on the words its own step types, an address part in no link and not in the goal, or a goal word written another way ("downloaded") sends the plan back in the same single ask, and the planner now sees where the page's links go. A step that only waited while its check already held (a "Buy" step on a page already showing "AAPL") is not sure, never a pass; an address typed only to make the step's address check hold is refused. A search on the way to a result may have no check of its own; the next step proves it, and a check there on the words the search typed is still flagged as proving little.
- **A bench.** `cli/bench` measures the tester with your AI on a local fixture site and a few public pages, measuring whether each answer was right in the browser and the fixture server, and compares two runs (`--compare`). It isn't part of `npm test`.

**Learn a product**

- `contextmachines learn <url>` drives a browser over the site: it follows links, opens menus and pop-ups, fills in forms with test values and presses their buttons.
- It writes `PRODUCT.md` (for people and coding agents), `map.json` (the same, as data) and a photo of each screen. `--html` adds `report.html`; `--json` prints the result for builds and agents.
- The same understanding the hosted product uses runs on your machine and says what the product is for, its screens, the actions people can take (with the request each sends), the main action, the setup steps it depends on and the main journeys.
- `--login` to sign in by hand first (only the session is kept, readable only by you), `--session` to reuse it.
- `--minutes` and `--pages` bound how long it explores and how far; a value out of range is refused, not changed.

**Stays read-only as far as it can show**

- Every save is answered on your machine: HTTP writes and GraphQL mutations are stopped in the browser, WebSocket messages reach the server only when they are known reads or upkeep, and pages get no shared workers.
- Addresses that sign out, delete or unsubscribe aren't followed, buttons that pay, buy, sign out or sign in elsewhere are never pressed, and cookie banners are declined.
- `--create` lets saves really happen, for test accounts, after a confirmation (`--yes` skips it). A write that says it destroys is still stopped.
- Secrets in the address you give it (a user name and password, a bypass token) are left out of everything it writes and prints.

**Issues it can prove**

- A button that does nothing, a page whose own script crashes, a request that fails, a same-site link that leads nowhere: only what the browser saw happen, each with its screen and evidence, in the terminal, `PRODUCT.md` and `map.json`.
- "Might trip people up": a few predictions of where a new person may get stuck, from plain rules over the map, always labelled as predictions and kept apart from the issues.

**Bring your own AI**

- Uses the first AI you already have: an Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, Vercel AI Gateway or OpenRouter key, any OpenAI-compatible server (Ollama, LM Studio, a company proxy), or a signed-in Claude Code or Codex. Choose with `--ai` and `--model`, or `--ai none`.
- The AI is sent labels, titles and the names of fields and requests, never a value or a photo. Its reading is reused while the product is unchanged (`--reading-cache` to share it between runs): no call, no cost, same words.

**What a change does**

- `contextmachines diff before/map.json after/map.json` writes markdown for a pull request comment (or `--json`): screens and actions added or gone, actions renamed or moved, a new main action, journeys a change breaks, and issues new or fixed. Predictions new or gone are listed apart, after them.
- A GitHub Action (`hughhellings/contextmachines/action@v0.1`) learns the app before and after a pull request and keeps one comment on it up to date, with the same in the job summary, the screen photos as an artifact, and `changed` and `issues` outputs. Its `v0.1` tag moves only to 0.1.x patch releases.

**For scripts and builds**

- Exit codes: 0 learned, 1 issues found with `--fail-on-issues` (or new ones with `diff --fail-on-new-issues`), 2 something typed wrong, 3 couldn't learn the site, 130 interrupted.
- `-v`, `--version`; `NO_COLOR` and `FORCE_COLOR`; `CONTEXTMACHINES_DEBUG=1` for the full error.
- A Node older than 22 gets a sentence saying so, not a stack trace.
